# *The Greenwood's Heart* — Airtight Outline

**Total target: ~10,000 words across 10 chapters**

---

## Chapter 1 — The Mother's Voice (~800 words)

**Setting:** Deep Greenwood, autumn 1897, golden hour. The heart tree clearing.

**POV:** Thistle.

**Beats:**
- Open on Thistle, human-sized in glamour, pressing her palm to the Mother's bark. She speaks in her formal cadence, reporting the day's tending: a vixen's den reinforced, a blight of rot turned back on the north slope.
- Establish her freckles-like-birch-bark and sun-through-leaves eyes through her reflection in a rain basin.
- Show her collection in a hollow at the Mother's base: a brass button, a child's bootlace, a tin whistle. Humans leave things at the forest's edge; she cannot help taking them.
- Aunt Morrow arrives as a crow, lands on a low branch, shifts to her half-form (woman's face, crow's black eyes, feathered shoulders). Warning #1: *"The Miller men have marked the eastern stakes. Axes by the new moon. Do not go near them, girl. I lost my Thomas to a musket and a smile."*
- Thistle's steel-cold voice emerges for the first time: *"If they come for the Mother, they will not leave."*
- Distant sound: a man's voice, far off, shouting once in pain. Then silence. Morrow caws a warning. Thistle hesitates — and goes.
- **Closing image:** Her bare feet on moss, moving east toward the sound, the tin whistle left behind on the Mother's roots.

**Planted for later:** The tin whistle (Samuel plays one in the epilogue). Morrow's Thomas (echoed in Morrow's Ch. 9 blessing). The formal cadence. The steel voice.

## Chapter 2 — Sorry, Sorry (~1,000 words)

**Setting:** Eastern edge of the Greenwood, dusk bleeding to full dark.

**POV:** Thistle, with a brief slip into Cal's delirium.

**Beats:**
- Thistle finds Cal pinned under a widow-maker branch — a rotten cedar limb the size of a horse across his left leg (the already-bad leg, re-broken). He's been there eight hours. Blood crusted, lips cracked, half-conscious.
- He is murmuring, over and over, to the tree that dropped the branch: *"Sorry. Sorry. Didn't mean to crowd you. Sorry."*
- This is the moment. Fae law says let the forest take him. Every rule she has says walk away. But he said *sorry* to a tree.
- She kneels. He opens his eyes — green-grey, unfocused — and whispers, *"Are you real?"* She answers, formal and shaking: *"I am as real as you are kind."*
- She lifts the branch with fae strength (underestimate-her payoff #1). She presses her palm to his leg; green light, the sound of growing things. The bone knits. The scar on his brow twitches.
- He sees, for one second, her wings unfurl behind her — iridescent, impossible. Then her glamour snaps back. He tells himself he's dying and seeing angels.
- She half-carries him to the tree line where his dog Bract (one-eared, frantic) finds them. Bract does not growl at her. Bract licks her hand. She laughs — wind-chime laugh — the first time in the story.
- She leaves him at the trailhead where the camp men will find him. Before she goes, he catches her wrist. *"Name?"* She cannot lie. *"Thistle."* *"I'm Cal."* *"I know."* She's watched him mark trees for three weeks and apologize to every one.
- **Closing image:** Thistle in the tree line, watching two lantern-bearing loggers carry Cal toward Miller's Cut. Her hand still warm where he held her wrist.

**Planted for later:** Bract's immediate trust. The wing-glimpse he'll "remember" at the midpoint kiss. Her watching him for three weeks (makes her glamour-meet-cute in Ch. 3 a deliberate choice, not a coincidence).

## Chapter 3 — The Kettle and the Straight Razor (~1,000 words)

**Setting:** Three days later. Cal's cabin — a one-room logger's cabin a half-mile from Miller's Cut, built by his father. Stove, bed, table, one chair, Bract's rag rug, a shelf of whittled birds.

**POV:** Cal.

**Beats:**
- Cal is healing impossibly fast. The camp doctor is baffled. He's walking already, limp almost gone. He knows what he saw. He isn't ready to say it.
- Dusk. A knock. A woman on his porch in a too-thin dress, barefoot, "lost." Ash-blonde hair, freckles in patterns he can't quite parse, eyes that wrong shade of green. She says her name is Thistle. He says nothing for a long moment. Then: *"Come in."*
- He is immediately, visibly suspicious — and immediately, quietly honorable. He cannot leave a woman in the woods at dark. He gives her the chair, the stew, his only blanket. He sits on the floor.
- Fish-out-of-water comedy, played tender:
  - She lifts the kettle lid and peers in like it's an oracle.
  - She picks up his straight razor with reverence; he gently takes it back: *"That'll take your finger off."* She replies: *"I would grow another."* He stares. She smiles. He decides she's joking.
  - She asks, formal and devastating: *"Do you love your work, Caleb Thorne?"* He chokes on his coffee. No one has asked him that in his life. He says, *"No."* It's the most honest thing he's said in three years.
- She steals his hat off the peg while he banks the stove. He sees. He says nothing. He almost smiles.
- Bract sleeps against her feet all night. Cal does not sleep.
- Morning: he walks her to the tree line because she insists she knows her way from there. She turns back once. *"Thank you for the stew, Caleb."* He blushes to his collar. She's gone before he can answer.
- **Closing image:** Cal returning to the cabin. His hat is back on the peg. A single iridescent scale, smaller than a fingernail, is on the chair where she sat. He puts it in his breast pocket.

**Planted for later:** The scale in his pocket (he'll touch it in Ch. 6 when he realizes). The hat-stealing (callback in the epilogue). *"I would grow another"* (echoed when she tells the truth in Ch. 9).

## Chapter 4 — Six Days (~1,200 words)

**Setting:** Cal's cabin and the Greenwood's edge, over six days.

**POV:** Alternating, tight — three Cal sections, three Thistle.

**Beats:**

*Day 1 (Cal):* She's back at dusk with a basket of chanterelles she *"found."* He makes stew again. She learns what butter is and cries a little. He pretends not to see.

*Day 2 (Thistle):* Morrow confronts her in crow form. Warning #2: *"He marks trees for the Miller Company, girl. Do you know what that means?"* Thistle, who has been so happy she forgot to ask, goes cold. She tells herself she'll ask him tomorrow. She doesn't.

*Day 3 (Cal):* He teaches her to hold an axe — the ironic-foreshadowing scene. His hands over hers on the haft. She is touch-starved; she goes very still. He notices. He steps back too fast and nearly falls over Bract. She laughs the wind-chime laugh. He dreams about it.

*Day 4 (Thistle):* She takes him to the hidden spring (not the Mother — she's not that foolish yet). He drinks. His limp, already better, disappears entirely. He looks at her and does not ask. She loves him for not asking.

*Day 5 (Cal):* He tells her, haltingly, about his father. The debt. Samuel. Three years of breaking his body to buy his brother a classroom. He has never said this aloud. He says it looking at the stove, not her. She says, *"Your heart is a good one, Caleb. I am sorry it has carried so much."* He has to go outside. He stands in the dark and breathes like he's been running.

*Day 6 (Thistle, ending the chapter):* She watches him split wood shirtless from the tree line (the trope, delivered). She does not understand what she is feeling. She understands she will do anything to keep it. That night, thunderstorm. One bed. He offers her the bed and takes the floor. She, who cannot lie, says, *"I do not wish to sleep alone."* He doesn't move for a full minute. Then he climbs in, fully clothed, and lies as far from her as the mattress allows. Neither sleeps. Their hands find each other in the dark between them.

- **Closing image:** Dawn. Their fingers still linked. Neither has moved.

**Planted for later:** The axe lesson (payoff in Ch. 7 when he drops it). The spring (where he'll carry her in Ch. 8). The *"your heart is a good one"* line (she'll say it again at the end of Ch. 9).

## Chapter 5 — The Cedar Sparrow (~900 words)

**Setting:** The cabin, the seventh morning. Light through the window.

**POV:** Cal.

**Beats:**
- He wakes first. Her hand in his. Her hair across his chest. He does not move for an hour. He is thirty years old and has never been this afraid.
- She wakes. They look at each other. He breaks first — the cinnamon roll cracks. He kisses her like he's apologizing for it. She kisses him back like she has been waiting two hundred years.
- Sensory beats: the smell of cedar smoke, her hair like cool water, his beard against her jaw. Outside the window, a cluster of late-autumn trillium blooms where none were yesterday. Neither sees.
- Her glamour flickers for half a breath — he glimpses wings, the true green of her eyes, freckles resolving into bark-patterns — and he closes his eyes and kisses her again. He chooses not to ask. She, who cannot lie, feels that choice like a vow.
- He gets up, rummages, comes back with something wrapped in a cloth. A small cedar sparrow, barely larger than a thimble, whittled over the last three nights while she slept. *"For you. It's not much."* She cannot speak. She closes her hand around it and does not open it again for two days.
- She resolves to tell him. Tonight. All of it. The Mother, the wings, her name-in-full. Fae cannot lie but they can delay, and she has delayed long enough.
- He leaves for Miller's Cut at midday to collect his week's pay and the new contract assignment. He kisses her at the door. *"Back by dark."* She watches him go, the cedar sparrow warm in her palm.
- **Closing image:** Thistle alone in the cabin, practicing what she will say. *"Caleb, I am not — Caleb, the forest is — Caleb, my name is Thistle-in-the-Hollow, and I —"* She cannot find the words. She will find them tonight.

**Planted for later:** The cedar sparrow (the token — in her hand through Ch. 7 and 8). The trillium blooming (echoed when the sapling rises in Ch. 8). The wing-glimpse he chose not to ask about.

## Chapter 6 — The Eastern Ridge (~1,000 words)

**Setting:** Cal's cabin, late afternoon into evening.

**POV:** Thistle, with one devastating paragraph in Cal's head.

**Beats:**
- Cal comes home early. He is lighter than she has ever seen him — almost smiling. He is carrying a bottle of something and a folded paper.
- *"Thistle. Thistle, it came through. The big one."* He unfolds the contract. She does not know how to read human writing well, but she knows the word *GREENWOOD* stamped at the top in red ink. She knows *EASTERN RIDGE*. She knows *HEART CEDAR — PRIORITY FELL.*
- He's talking. He can't stop talking, for once in his life. *"Six weeks' work. Clears the debt. Samuel comes home. I can — Thistle, I can stop. After this one, I can stop. I was going to ask you —"*
- He sees her face. He stops.
- One paragraph in his head: he touches his breast pocket, where the iridescent scale has lived for nine days. He feels it through the wool. He thinks, *oh.*
- She says, very quietly, very steel: *"Caleb. Do not go to the eastern ridge."*
- *"Thistle—"*
- *"Do not go. I am asking you. Do not go."*
- *"It's my brother. It's everything. I don't — why are you —"*
- She cannot lie. She cannot make him understand without telling him, and she cannot tell him now, not like this, not with the contract in his hand and his brother's name in his mouth. She chooses wrong. She flees.
- She is out the door and into the trees before he can cross the room. He calls her name twice. Bract whines at the doorway.
- **Closing image:** Cal on the porch, contract in one hand, the iridescent scale in the other, finally looking at it in the light. He says, to no one: *"Oh, God. Oh, God, what have I —"*

**Planted for later:** His realization (he goes to the ridge in Ch. 7 already knowing — which changes the black moment). The contract (burned in Ch. 8). Samuel's name (resolved in Ch. 9).

## Chapter 7 — The Axe and the Foreman (~1,200 words)

**Setting:** The heart-tree clearing, dawn. Mist. A dozen men with axes and a two-man saw. The Mother.

**POV:** Cal.

**Beats:**
- Cal has not slept. He is at the clearing before the crew. He stands in front of the Mother and looks up at her — a thousand years of red cedar, 250 feet tall, nine feet across. He has never seen her before. He understands, in his body, what he was about to do.
- The crew arrives. Hiram Beck is already sneering — he's seen Cal's face. *"Thorne. You look like hell. Pick up your axe."*
- Cal does not pick up his axe.
- Thistle steps out of the tree line. Full fae. Glamour gone. Wings unfurled, iridescent in the mist, six inches taller than her human form somehow — she is *terrible* and beautiful. The cedar sparrow in her fist.
- The crew recoils. Two men drop their tools and run. Bract, at Cal's heel, does not move.
- Hiram laughs — the ugly kind. *"A witch. Thorne's been tupping a witch. That's why you've gone soft, boy. That's why you've been weeping over every damn pine."* He spits. *"Miller Company don't pay for witchcraft. Pick up the axe or I'll have your contract and your brother's future in the fire by noon."*
- Cal, man of few words, says: *"Hiram. Step back from the tree."*
- *"The hell I will."*
- Hiram grabs Cal's axe off the ground — the same axe Cal taught Thistle to hold, six days ago — and swings at the Mother.
- The axe bites. Deep. A sound like a scream that isn't quite sound.
- Thistle falls. Just — falls. Like her strings are cut. The cedar sparrow rolls from her hand.
- Cal's silence finally breaks. He roars. He is on Hiram before the foreman can swing twice. One punch. Hiram goes down and does not get up. The remaining crew scatters.
- Cal is on his knees in the moss. Thistle is not breathing right. There is sap running from the Mother's wound and blood, somehow, at the corner of Thistle's mouth. He gathers her up — she weighs nothing, she has always weighed nothing — and he runs.
- **Closing image:** Cal running toward the hidden spring with Thistle in his arms, Bract at his heels, the cedar sparrow clenched in Cal's fist where he grabbed it from the moss.

**Planted for later:** Hiram unconscious, not dead (he'll be hauled off by the sheriff in Ch. 9's background, a clean loose end). The axe Cal taught her to hold being the axe that struck her — the ironic foreshadowing paid in full.

## Chapter 8 — A Named Sacrifice (~1,200 words)

**Setting:** The hidden spring. Midday. Sun through cedars.

**POV:** Cal.

**Beats:**
- He lays her at the spring's edge. Her freckles are fading. Her wings are dimming. He does not know the mythology — he only knows what she showed him here, six days ago, when the water took his limp away.
- Morrow arrives. Crow, then half-form. She is weeping, which Thistle has never seen her do. She tells him, fast and flat: *"The Mother's heartwood takes a named sacrifice. A human, freely. Your true name, three times, over her roots. And something that costs you. All of it. Or she dies, and my girl dies with her."*
- *"How long do I have?"*
- *"You are already too late, logger. Try anyway."*
- He runs back to the clearing with Thistle in his arms — he will not leave her, not even for this. Bract keeps pace. Morrow flies ahead, a black streak.
- At the Mother's roots, he lays Thistle against the trunk. He takes from his coat: the contract (folded, red-stamped), his flint, and the last $60 in Miller Company scrip — the money that was supposed to bring Samuel home.
- He burns the contract at the roots. *"Caleb Thorne."*
- He buries the scrip in the moss at the base of the wound. *"Caleb Thorne."* His voice breaks. He is giving up his brother's rescue. He is trusting a forest he's spent three years cutting to find another way.
- He draws his knife and opens his palm. He presses his bleeding hand to the axe-wound in the Mother's trunk. *"Caleb Thorne. I'm his. I'm hers. Take it. Take whatever. Just —"*
- The forest answers. Not gently. A sound like a great breath drawn in. The sap at the wound runs gold. The axe-cut closes, knits, scars. And from the scar — impossibly, immediately — a sapling, no taller than his hand, pushes out of the bark.
- Thistle does not wake. He waits. He does not move. Bract lies across her feet. Morrow watches from a branch and does not speak.
- An hour. Two. The light shifts.
- Then — her fingers twitch. Her eyes open. The green of sun through new leaves.
- He cannot speak. He puts the cedar sparrow back in her hand.
- **Closing image:** Thistle's fingers closing over the sparrow. The sapling at the Mother's scar. Cal's forehead pressed to Thistle's. Bract's tail, once, against the moss.

**Planted for later:** The sapling (waist-high in the epilogue). The gold sap (Morrow takes a vial; possible sequel hook, ignore if not wanted). The scrip buried in the roots (the forest's debt, paid back in Ch. 9).

## Chapter 9 — What the Forest Found (~800 words)

**Setting:** Three days later. The cabin. Then the trail down to Hollow Creek. Then the post office.

**POV:** Cal, then Thistle.

**Beats:**
- Thistle has been sleeping in his bed for three days. She wakes fully on the third morning. They have the conversation at last.
- She tells him everything. Her full name — Thistle-in-the-Hollow. Her two hundred years. The Mother. Fae cannot lie. The six days were the truest thing she has ever had. *"I would have told you. I was going to tell you that night. I delayed, and I am sorry, and I will be sorry for as long as I live, which is a very long time, Caleb."*
- He says, *"I knew. I knew by the end of day three. I didn't want to be right."* He takes the iridescent scale from his breast pocket and puts it in her palm. She laughs the wind-chime laugh and cries at the same time.
- He has to go down the mountain. The camp will be crawling with Miller men and sheriffs by now. He needs to face what he's done — broken contract, unconscious foreman, witnessed witchcraft. He may be arrested. He goes anyway. *"Your heart is a good one, Caleb,"* she says. *"I know. You taught me."*
- Hollow Creek. Miller Company office. He turns himself in for the contract break. Hiram, conscious and in jail for assault (three witnesses turned on him), has been ranting about witches; no one believes him. Cal pays the $60 debt — *he doesn't have $60, but he offers to work it off* — and the clerk stops him. *"Thorne. There's mail for you. Two weeks old. Your brother."*
- The letter: Samuel won the territorial essay prize. $200 and a teacher's apprenticeship in Portland. Room and board included. He starts in the spring. *"I can send money home now, Cal. You can stop. Please stop."*
- Cal stands in the post office and cries, which he has not done since he was eight. Mae Pritchard, behind the counter, hands him a handkerchief and does not say anything unkind. She has been engaged to the blacksmith for a month. She wishes him well. She means it.
- He walks back up the mountain. The forest is quiet in the way forests are when they are watching.
- **Closing image:** Thistle on the cabin porch at dusk, waiting. Cal coming up the path. Bract, ahead of him, already running.

**Planted for later:** Mae's handkerchief (tiny grace note; she appears once more in the epilogue, happy). Samuel's letter (he visits in the epilogue).

## Chapter 10 — The Sapling (~500 words)

**Setting:** One year later. Late autumn 1898. The cabin, the clearing, the sapling.

**POV:** Cal, with the final paragraph in Thistle's head.

**Beats:**
- The cabin has a second room now. Cal built it himself, badly, and Thistle loves it.
- He is the warden of the Greenwood — paid by the Cascade Conservation Society, a group Samuel connected him with through his teaching network in Portland. Forty dollars a month to walk the woods and keep the Miller men out. He cannot believe it is a job. He is extremely good at it.
- Samuel visits for a week every summer. He has met Thistle. He has accepted, with a schoolteacher's careful curiosity, what she is. He plays the tin whistle Thistle left on the Mother's roots a year ago; she returned it to him at his first visit. He does not ask how she knew.
- Bract has grey on his muzzle and sleeps most of the day. He still will not let Thistle leave the cabin without him.
- Mae Pritchard married the blacksmith and is expecting a child at Christmas. She sent a pie. Cal ate it without irony.
- Morrow visits every full moon. She is still bitter, but she calls Cal *"logger"* now instead of something worse, and once — once — she let him bring her a cup of tea. She did not drink it. She did not throw it, either.
- The sapling at the Mother's scar is waist-high. It grows a foot a month. Thistle says it is the Mother's daughter. She says it has Cal's name in its roots, where the scrip was buried, where his blood soaked in.
- Final scene: Cal on the porch at dusk, whittling. A second cedar sparrow, smaller than the first. Thistle beside him, his hat on her head, her hand on her stomach. She has not told him yet. She will tonight.
- **Closing image, Thistle's POV:** She watches his hands work the cedar. She thinks: *I have been alive for two hundred years and I did not know I was lonely until he said sorry to a tree.* She puts her hand over his. He looks up. He almost smiles.
